Murat Atimtay is a computational artist and creative technologist exploring the relationships between people, technology, and the systems that connect them. He is drawn to the rituals we form around machines and the ways we find meaning, presence, and connection in invisible digital processes.

His work has been shown in Times Square, London, and Frankfurt, as well as at the Lights in Jerusalem Festival, where he presented an interactive installation in 2017. In 2019, his algorithmic tattoo project, Heliantone, received first prize at the Frankfurt Tattoo Convention.

He continues to work in generative art, pen plotter drawing, and interactive installations, and teaches creative coding to children and adults through Kodart.
